SOURCE OF EVERY ROW Kiosk check-ins and PIN signatures · spoken and tapped meal logs · drill and medication events. Nothing on this page was entered for the sake of this page.
  1. Reports are grouped by the situation that sends someone here, not alphabetically. Nobody opens this screen wanting “the A report” — they open it because a licensor is in the doorway, a subsidy claim is due, or a parent is disputing hours. The three headings are those three moments, so she scans for her situation rather than for a filename.
  2. The screen shows the page, outlined and margined, at the proportions it will actually print at. These documents end up in a three-ring binder, in a licensor's hand, or stapled to a subsidy claim, so the printed artifact is the deliverable and the screen is only a preview of it. A screen layout that gets reflowed at print time would mean nobody ever sees the thing they are handing over until it is already in someone else's hand.
  3. Letterhead is generated from the account — business name, licence number, date range, generated timestamp, page numbering. A provider should never open a word processor to make a document look official, because the moment she does, the official-looking version and the true version start to drift apart.
  4. The print layer is designed rather than inherited: landscape page sizing so the compliance columns fit, the table scaled to the page rather than clipped, and page-break-inside: avoid so a child's record never splits across two sheets. A record broken over a page turn is the one an inspector reads as missing.
  5. Dark mode is neutralised for print. A provider printing from a dark screen still gets black on white, because getting a licensing document wrong on account of a theme preference is exactly the class of bug that never shows up in a design review and always shows up on paper.
  6. The compliance artifacts stopped being separate work. Attendance records, signed releases, CACFP claims and fire-drill logs are all generated from events already captured for another reason — she logged a check-in, not a report line. That is the whole argument for capturing events at the moment they happen rather than reconstructing them at month end.
