This tier was retired in the project’s final commit. The screen shipped, sold, and is no longer live.
  1. A cheaper manual-entry plan was given its own home screen rather than the Voiceboard with the microphone taken out. What this plan sells is a clean daily record with almost no setup, not the absence of voice, so the screen opens on the three things a provider actually does — and the columns beneath repeat those three verbs in the same order, so whatever she just logged appears directly under the button she pressed.
  2. Voiceboard, Logged Events and Calendar are absent from the navigation rather than padlocked. A padlock is honest for Custom Forms or Expenses, which this account can buy this afternoon; holding the flagship feature up in front of an account whose plan cannot include it at all just reads as a nag.
  3. Every event the full product captures by speech is capturable by hand here — same event types, same table, same rows feeding attendance and the CACFP claim at month end. The data model and the compliance outputs are identical across the two tiers, and only the capture surface differs.
  4. A running entry has no end time, so it cannot be a row like the ones below it. It gets a live counter and a single Complete button pinned above the closed rows, because the failure manual entry invites is a day that quietly never got closed and is noticed a fortnight later.
  5. This has to beat a paper notebook, not the voice tier. Two required choices, a time already filled in, and a save — anything longer and she writes it on the back of an envelope and enters it at 8pm from memory, which is the exact habit the product exists to end.
  6. Drawing a retired screen is deliberate. The tier proved the record rather than the microphone was the thing being bought, and it is the case study's clearest example of a pricing idea that did not hold — worth saying plainly rather than presenting the plan as though it were still on sale.
