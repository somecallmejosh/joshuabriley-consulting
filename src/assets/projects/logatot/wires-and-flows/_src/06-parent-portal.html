The email is the product. The portal is the destination.
Session shape A parent signs in perhaps a dozen times a year, one-handed, in a car park at five o’clock. The portal is designed for the visit that is already rare rather than for the habit nobody forms.
  1. A switcher, not a list of children. About a third of enrolled families have more than one child in care, and a parent checks both in the same half minute — so switching swaps the whole page rather than costing a navigation step and a scroll back.
  2. The counts sit above the timeline and stay deliberately small. Arrival, meals, naps and diapers are what a parent needs to plan dinner and bedtime; they are reference, and reference should not outweigh the day itself.
  3. Exactly the events the Voiceboard renders, turned ninety degrees. She is scanning twelve children at once, so she gets a grid; a parent is reading about one child in order, so they get a chronology with time down the left. Same query, opposite affordance.
  4. Photos and milestones get a heavier border and a filled node on the rail, so they survive a thumb-scroll. A diaper count is a compliance record; a photo is the entry a parent screenshots and sends to a grandparent, and it is why they opened the app at all.
  5. Read-mostly, with exactly one write. Messaging is the only action a parent can take here — no editing, no logging, no reactions — because every extra write surface becomes something the provider has to triage during the day, and she is already holding a toddler.
  6. Nobody forms a habit of opening a web app for a toddler's nap times, so the summary mails itself on check-out and carries the day's photos in the preview. Tapping through is a bonus. The link is also the sign-in: a password set in March is forgotten by June, so the reset flow became the only flow.
