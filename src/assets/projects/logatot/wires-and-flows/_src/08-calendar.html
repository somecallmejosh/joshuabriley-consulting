Where this record goes Rolls up into Reports → Fire drill log, twelve months per page, one row per drill with participants, time to exit and notes. Nothing is retyped. Print styles neutralise the theme — dark:print:text-black — so a report printed from a dark screen still hands over black on white.
Cancel Save drill record
  1. A home daycare is planned around closures — holidays, vacation weeks, a licensing visit — so month is the default view and week is the exception. Nobody opens a calendar to find out what today is; they open it to answer a parent asking whether they are open the week of the 20th.
  2. Event type is carried by line style rather than colour: solid for an ordinary event, dashed for a fire drill, dotted for shelter-in-place. It reads on a month sheet printed and taped to the fridge, and it survives the fact that plenty of providers print in black and white. The legend earns its place because line style is learned, where colour is guessed.
  3. A fire drill is not an appointment, so fire_drill and shelter_in_place are distinct task types with their own typed detail records. The fields a drill carries — who took part, how long it took to get everyone out, what went wrong — are exactly what a licensor asks for, and a plain calendar event has nowhere to put them.
  4. Participants are the roster ticked off, not a free-text list of names. The question a year later is whether one specific child was in the building for one specific drill, and only a structured record answers it. Ticking all present is one control because that is the true case nine times out of ten.
  5. Time to full exit is a field with a timer beside it rather than a line in a notes box, and the notes field becomes required once the time crosses the limit. Logging the drill and proving the drill are the same action here — the alternative is a provider reconstructing the number from memory the week the licensor is due.
  6. The compliance report is assembled from these records rather than typed, so print is a designed surface rather than a media-query afterthought. Getting a licensing document wrong because of a theme preference is the class of bug that never turns up in design review.
