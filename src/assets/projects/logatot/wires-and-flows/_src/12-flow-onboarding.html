User Flow — Provider Onboarding

The empty board is the churn risk, not the signup form. Activation is where the flow starts, not where it ends.

LOGATOT USER FLOW
A provider decides to try it Twelve children, a paper attendance sheet, no software of any kind
Signup form Her name, the daycare’s name, email, password — about ninety seconds
One submit writes three records User, daycare account and provider, in a single transaction
She is one person, not three rows A solo operator does not think of herself as a user, an account and a member of staff. The data model can carry all three as long as the form never mentions them.

The board loads, and it is empty No children, no rows, nothing to say a name at
The empty board is the real churn risk, not the signup form. Signup takes ninety seconds. Entering twelve children by hand is the wall, and an account that never clears it never sees the product work.

Entry / terminal Step System step Branch User choice Exit Design note
  1. Signup is ninety seconds and is treated as the cheap early step rather than the goal — one form, one submit, three linked records written together. Spending more design effort here would be polishing the part that already works, and the flow deliberately puts eleven steps after it.
  2. Activation branches because the money arrives through more than one door. Membership type, sponsor relationship, beta code and subscription date are each independently sufficient, so a card is one of four signals rather than the gate. A meaningful share of accounts come through a state or national association, and treating those as exceptions bolted onto Stripe would mean special-casing every association at checkout instead of once, at entitlement.
  3. Time to first value is not “account created”. It is “the board has children on it and a spoken event has landed in a row”, which is why that is the terminal state of this sheet and account creation sits near the top as a cheap step. The empty board is where the flow either completes or quietly stops.
  4. This is the branch that loses accounts. Twelve forms is not hard, it is long, and a provider who stops at the fourth child now has an entitlement, an empty-ish board and no reason to open it again. Drawing the stall as a real outcome rather than assuming completion is what made import worth building at all.
  5. CSV import sits on the roster header beside Add child rather than in settings, because a provider signing up already has twelve children written down somewhere from her licensing renewal. In settings it would be found by people who already know it exists — which is not the people stalling at the fourth form.
  6. Adding a child is also what makes voice work: each name goes to a background job that pre-computes its phonetic aliases before the matcher ever needs them. So the roster import is not data entry ahead of the product, it is the product being configured — and a board filled by hand and a board filled by CSV arrive at exactly the same state.
