  1. The email is the product and the portal is the destination. Nobody forms a habit of opening a web app to check a toddler's nap times, so the summary mails itself on check-out and carries the day's emotional payload in the preview — the photo, the long nap, the fact that she ate everything. A parent who reads it and never taps through has still had the whole experience, which is why this branch is drawn as an ending rather than a drop-off.
  2. A parent signs in perhaps a dozen times a year, and at that frequency a password is a support ticket waiting to happen. Since the reset path was going to be a mailed link regardless, keeping a password field as well would mean maintaining two credentials where one of them is never remembered. Email possession is the credential, and the form shrinks to a single field.
  3. Both branches land on the same screen, and that convergence is the security property rather than a layout convenience. A form that says “no account found” is a free tool for testing whether a particular family attends a particular daycare — the mailer is called only when a user exists, but the response never lets on either way.
  4. Fifteen minutes and one use is strict enough to survive a forwarded email or a shared inbox, and strictness like that is only tolerable if recovery is trivial. So the expired state is a button rather than an apology, it does not ask for the address again, and it loops straight back into the flow — which is the difference between a security decision and a lockout.
  5. One door, routed by whatever the token resolved to. Two login pages guarantees that a parent eventually arrives at the staff one and bounces off it, and a member of staff whose own child attends would need two habits and two addresses. The destination is a consequence of the matched record, not something the person has to know before they start.
