  1. A kiosk mode layered inside a live session is one stray back-gesture away from a parent reading another family's notes. So the handoff signs the provider out and clears the session entirely — there is nothing left to escape into, and nothing a parent taps leads anywhere but the PIN screen. The account id is the one thing kept, which is what makes coming back a single tap rather than a penalty for having handed the tablet over at all.
  2. A password assumes two free hands and a device the parent has used before. She has a car seat on one arm and someone behind her, so it is four digits and no submit button — the child list appears as she finishes typing. Taking the submit step out removes the only place the interaction could stall, and filtering the list to that PIN's children means a neighbour collecting one child never learns who else attends.
  3. A wrong PIN is a tired parent, not an intruder, so it is not treated as a security event. The digits clear, the keypad stays, there is no lockout and no accusatory copy. The real fallback is standing two feet away and can check the child out herself — designing for the attacker here would mostly punish the customer.
  4. Who may collect a child is a custody question, and custody belongs in a record that can be audited rather than in the provider remembering who is allowed to take whom. An authorized pickup is a first-class user with their own PIN and their own permitted children, so a grandparent sees only the children they may take, and the release is attributable to them by name six months later.
  5. The signature is worth having only because of when it was taken. Captured on the canvas while the parent is still standing there and attached to that one attendance event, it answers the question a licensing review actually asks: who released this child, and when. A stack of signatures collected at close of day is easier to build and proves nothing about any particular release.
  6. Nothing on this path writes an attendance flag. Present-or-not is computed by reading the latest check-in, check-out or absent event for that child on that date. Voice, the kiosk, the board and the API are four write paths, and a stored flag drifts the moment any one of them forgets to update it — the event log is the only thing all four already agree on. It costs query complexity and buys a headcount that cannot silently be wrong.
