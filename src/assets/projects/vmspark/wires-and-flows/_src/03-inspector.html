Reprocessing re-runs mapping and the rule engine for this listing only. Add Note Reprocess This Listing
  1. The inspector opens over the filtered list rather than navigating away from it, and it fetches on open — nothing about this panel is on the hot path, so it costs first paint nothing. Triage is a run of twelve failures, and losing the filter and scroll position between each one is what turns a ten-minute pass into an afternoon.
  2. A FAILED chip on the grid says something went wrong; this says which field the mapper could not resolve, in the operator's own words rather than a stack trace. The whole reason this screen exists is that the alternative was a support ticket and someone grepping the import log.
  3. Raw payload beside the mapped result, because those are the two things in dispute whenever a listing is wrong — did the VMS send us rubbish, or did we mishandle good data. Keeping the inbound JSON means that stays answerable weeks after the import that caused it, not just while the failure is fresh.
  4. The description and the access link are collapsed. They are the bulk of the payload and almost never the thing being diagnosed, so they stay reachable without pushing the comparison and the history below the fold.
  5. Timestamped stages turn "it failed" into a sequence with a point of departure — imported fine, mapped nine of ten, fell over on the tenth. That ordering is what tells an operator whether to fix a mapping or chase the VMS feed.
  6. Read-mostly on purpose. The only writes here are reprocess and a note; editing the mapping happens on the mapping screen, reached from the banner above. A second editing surface would drift from the first, and then nobody would know which one the pipeline actually reads.
