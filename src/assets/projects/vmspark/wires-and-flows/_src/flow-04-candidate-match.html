  1. An unmapped profession or specialty is not a tidiness problem, it is a lost placement. The candidate and the requisition are never compared at all, so nobody sees a gap — the match simply does not exist, and no recruiter can miss what was never proposed. This is the clearest place mapping debt shows up as revenue.
  2. Credential state is checked before a match is proposed rather than at submission. A lapsed licence makes a candidate unplaceable however well she fits, and the expensive version of that discovery is a recruiter who has already made the call, sold the shift and then finds out.
  3. The run stores which criteria matched and which did not, not just a total. A bare score gives a recruiter nothing to act on; “bill rate $8 over the cap” tells her whether to argue the rate or move on, which is what turns a ranked list into a work queue.
  4. A match arrives at status New in the same queue as a human application, and nothing advances it on its own. The system is allowed to have an opinion about who fits — it is not allowed to act on one, because the cost of a wrong submission lands on the facility relationship rather than on the algorithm.
  5. Rejecting is deliberately cheap: it costs one click, writes nothing outward, and the candidate is back in tonight’s run. Making the reversible action as light as possible is what stops a recruiter treating submission as the safe default when she is working forty matches.
  6. Submission is the only step here that is visible outside the product and cannot be taken back in-app, so it is the only one that asks. Everything before it stays one click, which is the rule the whole flow is built on — make the correct path easier than the incorrect one rather than warning about both equally.
