  1. None of the three journeys below survives a stale mapping, and an unmapped value never fails loudly — the listing simply does not match, so nobody is ever told the assignment existed. That is why the mapping queue sits above this sheet rather than inside one band: it is a precondition on all three, not a task belonging to any of them.
  2. The operator's unit of work is a queue, not a record. She is triaging thousands of listings across a dozen VMS sources on a wide screen, so her grid earns density, keyboard operation and an inspector that opens beside the row instead of replacing it. Every affordance here is bought with screen width she actually has.
  3. Impersonation is a first-class journey, not an admin escape hatch — VMSpark staff spend real hours inside customer accounts. So the workspace switcher and the amber return-to-owner control live in the app shell on every screen rather than on an admin page. A mode you can enter and then forget you are in is worse than no mode at all.
  4. The recruiter works people, not listings. Same records, different verbs: she filters, reads and submits, and never sees the failed queue that fills the operator's day. Scoping her to her own tenant is what lets both live in one application instead of two, and it is the only reason the shell can be shared at all.
  5. Every journey ends in the ATS rather than in VMSpark. The product is a pipeline between two systems of record, so "done" is never a saved form here — it is a state, a submission or an application that has landed somewhere else and can be seen there by someone who never logs into this product.
  6. The clinician is the operator's opposite: one assignment at a time, on a phone, between shifts, with no login and no shell around her. Serving her from the same dense grid would have compromised both of them, which is why the candidate portal is rebuilt rather than reskinned from the operator's tables.
