## What I Do

### I help teams improve the systems behind the experience.

Product teams often struggle in the space between an idea and a shipped interface. Designs look polished in Figma, but the implementation becomes inconsistent. Components exist, but teams do not know how to use them. Accessibility is discussed late. Documentation answers the wrong questions. Small interface decisions pile up until the product feels harder to use than it should.

I help teams close those gaps.

### Clarify the experience

I help turn confusing flows, vague requirements, and inconsistent interface decisions into clearer product experiences. That includes reviewing user journeys, identifying friction, simplifying interaction patterns, and helping teams make decisions that are easier for users to understand.

### Strengthen the system

I help improve the reusable pieces that shape the product experience. That can include component behavior, accessibility guidance, design tokens, documentation, usage rules, and the patterns teams rely on every day.

### Bridge design and engineering

I help reduce the translation loss between design intent and implementation. I work comfortably with designers, engineers, product owners, and stakeholders to make sure interface decisions are not only thoughtful, but buildable, maintainable, and accessible.

## Why This Work Matters

### The interface is where strategy becomes real.

A product can have a smart roadmap, strong research, and talented teams, but users only experience what makes it to the screen.

That means small details matter.

A button label can reduce confusion. A form pattern can prevent errors. A focus state can make a workflow usable. A design system decision can save dozens of teams from solving the same problem in different ways.

Good UX is not only about big ideas. It is also about the small, repeated choices that shape how a product feels over time.

My work focuses on those choices.

## How I Think

### I care about the space between people, patterns, and code.

I started as a designer who learned to code. Over time, that turned into years of building interfaces, component libraries, and design systems for real product teams.

That background taught me something important: experience problems are rarely just design problems or engineering problems. They usually live in the handoff, the assumptions, the missing details, the unclear patterns, and the places where teams think they agree but do not.

I like working in that space.

I ask questions like:

- Is this interaction clear to the user?
- Does the interface behave the way people expect?
- Can teams reuse this pattern without misusing it?
- Are we solving the actual experience problem, or only making the screen look better?
- Will this still make sense when another team has to build on it six months from now?

That is the work I want to keep doing.
